1.2 DB0004

1.3 Project outline

This project aims to identify BRCA2-related proteins across four species Nasalis larvatus, Plecturocebus cupreus, Saguinus oedipus, and Homo sapiens (positive control). The workflow begins with BLASTp searches using the human BRCA2 protein sequence against the predicted protein sets of all four genomes to identify possible BRCA2-like polypeptides.

After BRCA2 proteins are identified, their corresponding transcript IDs was retrieved from the GTF annotation files. Because this analysis is based on protein queries, transcript analysis depends on the presence of annotated CDS features in the GTF. Any transcripts found was then used with BLASTN to locate their corresponding genomic regions and identify conserved BRCA2 loci within each genome.

1.4 Introduction

Breast Cancer Type 2 Susceptibility Protein (BRAC 2) is an essential DNA-repair gene that maintains genome stability through its role in homologous recombination (Venkitaraman 2002). Because BRCA2 function is highly conserved across mammals, comparative genomic studies have increasingly examined how BRCA2 structure and exon organization vary among species to better understand evolutionary constraints and functional domains (Zhang et al. 2011; Kowalczyk et al. 2020). Previous research has shown that although the BRCA2 protein is conserved, several primate genomes contain incomplete, fragmented, or poorly annotated BRCA2 regions due to assembly gaps or annotation limitations (Chen et al. 2017). These issues make cross-species comparison valuable for improving gene annotation and understanding BRCA2 evolution.

In this project, we perform a systematic comparative analysis of BRCA2 across four species—Homo sapiens, Nasalis larvatus, Saguinus oedipus, and Plecturocebus cupreus using BLASTp, gene annotation files, CDS extraction, and BLASTn-based genomic mapping. Our goal is to identify BRCA2 orthologs, evaluate their conservation, and determine whether the gene is fully represented, fragmented, or missing in each assembly. By using the human BRCA2 protein as a reference and mapping CDS back to each species genome, this analysis helps verify gene presence, detect structural differences, and highlight annotation gaps—an approach commonly used in evolutionary genomics and functional gene validation.

1.4.2 About the pipeline used

The pipeline follows 3 steps:

  1. BLASTp search (protein level): The human BRCA2 protein sequence was used as the reference query against the predicted protein sets from each species. This step identified potential BRCA2 homologs based on sequence similarity and alignment statistics.
  2. Transcript and CDS identification (annotation level): Protein hits were matched to their corresponding transcripts using GTF/GFF annotation files. This step enabled extraction of the coding sequences (CDS) and provided information on exon structure and completeness.
  3. Genomic mapping using BLASTn (genome level): The extracted CDS sequences were aligned to each species’ genome using BLASTn. Genomic hits were visualized in IGV to confirm exon organization and BRCA2 locus completeness.

1.4.3 About experimental rationale

The main goal of this project is to determine whether BRCA2-related proteins can be identified across four primate species. BRCA2 is an important gene involved in DNA repair, and detecting similar proteins in other species provides information into how conserved this gene is across evolution.

A protein-based approach was used because protein sequences are more conserved than nucleotide sequences, making BLASTp more effective for identifying distant homologs. Once a BRCA2-like protein is identified, annotation files such as GTFs help determine whether the gene and its transcripts are recorded in the species’ genome assembly. Finally, aligning the protein back to the genome using BLASTN allows the BRCA2 region to be located, helping us understand the organization and conservation of this gene in each species.

1.5 Methods and Results

1.5.1 Methods

  1. Preparation of protein databases
    • Protein FASTA files for Homo sapiens, Nasalis larvatus, Saguinus oedipus, and Plecturocebus cupreus were collected and converted into BLASTp-searchable databases using the makeblastdb command (-dbtype prot). This allowed protein–protein comparisons across species.
  2. Query sequence
    • The human BRCA2 protein sequence (ENSP00000518983.1) was downloaded from PEP file and used as the query for all BLASTp searches.This sequence served as the basis for identifying BRCA2-like proteins in the other primates.
  3. BLASTp search
    • BLASTp was run with default parameters to compare the human BRCA2 protein against each species protein database. The goal was to identify BRCA2-like proteins based on sequence similarity, alignment score, and E-value.
  4. Transcript and genome annotation
    • For each BLASTp hit, the species GFF annotation file was searched to identify the associated transcript and its CDS. Using the BLASTp-identified protein as a reference, the BRCA2 CDS was extracted from each species CDS FASTA file. These CDS sequences were then used as BLASTn queries to determine where the BRCA2 gene is located within each species genome.
  5. Genomic localization of BRCA2 using BLASTn and IGV
    • Each species genome FASTA file (.fna) was linked into the working directory and converted into a nucleotide BLAST database. The extracted BRCA2 CDS sequences were then aligned back to their respective genomes using BLASTn to locate the scaffold or chromosome containing the BRCA2 region. BLASTn output was converted into BED files and loaded into IGV, where the genomic position and conservation of BRCA2 were visually compared across species.

1.5.2 Results

1. BLASTp hit summary

BLASTp searches using the human BRCA2 protein (ENSP00000518983.1) detected BRCA2-like polypeptides in three of the four species analyzed. For each species, the best hit (lowest E-value) was used to summarize similarity. Percent identity, alignment length, and E-value were used to assess how closely each predicted protein resembles the human BRCA2 sequence.

  • For Homo sapiens: In Homo sapiens, BRCA2 was recovered with a perfect alignment showing 100% identity and full-length coverage of 3366 amino acids, with an E-value of 0.0. This confirms the presence of the complete BRCA2 protein and validates the BLASTp search setup.
  • For Nasalis larvatus: In Nasalis larvatus, a strong BRCA2 homolog was detected, showing 90.829% identity across a 3424 amino acid alignment, with an E-value of 0.0. This indicates that BRCA2 is highly conserved in this species and that the predicted protein set contains a full-length BRCA2 gene.
  • For Saguinus oedipus: In Saguinus oedipus, BLASTp detected BRCA2-like regions with a top hit showing 87.317% identity across a 205 amino acid segment, with an E-value of 1.52e-113. These shorter alignments suggest that the BRCA2 region is only partially represented, likely due to incomplete or fragmented protein annotation in this genome.
  • For Plecturocebus cupreus: No BLASTp hits were detected at any E-value, likely due to missing or incomplete BRCA2 annotation in the predicted protein set rather than biological absence. Further BLASTN searches against the genome may be required to recover the BRCA2 region.

2. BRCA2 CDS extraction and annotation summary

After identifying the BRCA2-like proteins using BLASTp, the corresponding CDS features were located in each species annotation files (GFF + CDS FASTA). Using the protein IDs, full CDS sequences were successfully extracted for three species:

  • Homo sapiens (ENSP00000518983.1): The CDS corresponding to the human BRCA2 transcript (ENST00000713680.1) was recovered in full, confirming the integrity of the annotation. The extracted FASTA file was saved as: BRCA2_Homo_ENSP00000518983.1_cds.fa.
  • Nasalis larvatus (KAL4838279.1): The BRCA2-like protein was traced to scaffold CM106352.1. Multiple CDS entries were identified spanning ~25 exon regions. The complete predicted BRCA2 CDS was extracted and saved as: BRCA2_Nasalis_KAL4838279.1_cds.fa.
  • Saguinus oedipus (KAK2112680.1): CDS features were located on scaffold CM063173.1. The extracted sequence represented a partial BRCA2 CDS, consistent with the shorter BLASTp alignment. The FASTA file was saved as: BRCA2_Saguinus_KAK2112680.1_cds.fa.
  • Plecturocebus cupreus: No CDS sequence could be extracted because no BRCA2-like protein was detected in the BLASTp search. This suggests incomplete genome annotation.

1.5.3 Step01: BLASTp analysis for BRCA2 protein

  1. Step01A: Preparing protein databases
    • Protein FASTA files for all four species were downloaded and converted into BLASTp databases using the 'makeblastdb' command with the '-dbtype prot' option.
    • Four directories were created: 01_Homo_sapiens_DB, 02_Nasalis_larvatus_DB, 03_Saguinus_oedipus_DB, 04_Plecturocebus_cupreus_DB
  2. Step01B: Running BLASTp
    • The human BRCA2 protein sequence (ENSP00000518983.1.fa) was used as the query.
    • BLASTp was run against each species' protein database to identify proteins with similarity to the human BRCA2 sequence.
    • Alignment scores, percent identity, and E-values were recorded for the top hits.
  3. Step01C: Processing BLASTp results
    • BLASTp output files (outfmt 0 and 6) were examined to identify the best BRCA2-like protein in each species.
    • Protein IDs and their percent identity, alignment length, and E-value were extracted for transcript and genome analyses.

1.5.4 Step02: Transcript extraction and genomic mapping of BRCA2 CDS

  1. Step02A: Identifying BRCA2 CDS from annotation files
    • For each top BLASTp hit, the corresponding species GFF annotation file was searched using the protein ID (ENSP00000518983.1, KAL4838279.1, KAK2112680.1) to find the associated gene, transcript, and CDS features.
    • These GFF entries were used to record the scaffold/chromosome name, strand, and exon coordinates for the BRCA2-like gene in each species.

    • Using the protein IDs as keys, the BRCA2 CDS was extracted from each species’ CDS FASTA file:

      • Homo sapiens: Homo_sapiens.GRCh38.cds.all.fa
      • Nasalis larvatus: GCA_047834645.1_ASM4783464v1_cds_from_genomic.fna
      • Saguinus oedipus: GCA_031835075.1_ASM3183507v1_cds_from_genomic.fna

      These CDS regions were saved as:

      • BRCA2_Homo_ENSP00000518983.1_cds.fa (Homo sapiens)
      • BRCA2_Nasalis_KAL4838279.1_cds.fa (Nasalis larvatus)
      • BRCA2_Saguinus_KAK2112680.1_cds.fa (Saguinus oedipus)
  2. Step02B: Building genomic BLASTn databases
    • Genomic FASTA files (.fna) for each species were linked into the working directory (Homo_sapiens_genomic_chr13.fa, Nasalis_larvatus_genomic.fa, Saguinus_oedipus_genomic.fa) using symbolic links (`ln -s`).
    • These genome FASTA files were converted into nucleotide BLAST databases using `makeblastdb` with `-dbtype nucl`, producing:
      • Homo_sapiens_chr13_db
      • Nasalis_larvatus_genome.fa_db
      • Saguinus_oedipus_genome.fa_db
  3. Step02C: BLASTn mapping and IGV visualization
    • Each BRCA2 CDS FASTA file was used as a BLASTn query against the appropriate genome database to determine the BRCA2 genomic locus for each species.
    • BLASTn results were generated in tabular format (outfmt 6), sorted, and converted into BED files (sorted_*Run_12*.bed) to capture genomic coordinates of high-scoring alignments.
    • Genome FASTA files and the BED tracks were transferred to Windows using `rsync` and loaded into IGV.
    • In IGV, the BRCA2 region was inspected on:
      • Human chromosome 13 (full BRCA2 locus)
      • Nasalis larvatus scaffold CM106352.1
      • Saguinus oedipus scaffold CM063173., to visually compare BRCA2 genomic structure across species.

1.6.1 Making BLASTp databases

1.9 Discussion

The main goal of this project was to identify BRCA2 homologs across four primate species and compare how similar they are at the protein level, how complete their CDS regions are, and how their BRCA2 gene is organized in the genome. From the BLASTp search, strong BRCA2-like proteins were recovered in Homo sapiens, Nasalis larvatus, and Saguinus oedipus. However, no BRCA2 protein was detected in Plecturocebus cupreus, which is most likely due to missing or incomplete annotations rather than the actual absence of the gene. This is a common issue with non-model genomes that may still have gaps or incomplete protein predictions (Lewin et al. 2018). Across the species, the amount of BRCA2 conservation varied noticeably. Nasalis larvatus had a highly conserved BRCA2 protein (~91% identity) and the alignment covered almost the entire protein, showing that BRCA2 is well preserved in this species. On the other hand, Saguinus oedipus only had short BRCA2-like fragments (~87% identity over 205 amino acids). This likely reflects gaps in the genome assembly or fragmented protein prediction rather than true biological differences. Large genes like BRCA2, which contains many exons and spans more than 10 kb, are especially sensitive to incomplete genome assemblies (Roy et al. 2023).The CDS extraction and GFF annotation results support this pattern. In humans and Nasalis larvatus, the CDS regions were complete and showed the expected multi-exon structure of BRCA2. Saguinus oedipus only had partial CDS segments, again pointing toward incomplete annotation. IGV visualization also matched these findings: humans showed a full-length BRCA2 region, Nasalis showed many clean exon hits along the scaffold, and Saguinus displayed fragmented alignments. Together, these results highlight how important genome quality is when identifying and comparing genes across species.Overall, this project demonstrates how BLAST-based comparative genomics can help reveal evolutionary patterns, annotation completeness, and how genes like BRCA2 are structured in different primates.

1.10 Conclusions

This project successfully identified and compared BRCA2 homologs across four primate species using BLAST-based protein, CDS, and genomic analyses. The results showed that BRCA2 is highly conserved in Homo sapiens and Nasalis larvatus, with both species containing complete and well-annotated versions of the gene. In contrast, Saguinus oedipus only showed partial BRCA2 sequences, and Plecturocebus cupreus did not show any detectable BRCA2 protein. These outcomes strongly suggest that the missing or fragmented BRCA2 regions are due to incomplete genome annotation rather than true evolutionary loss. The combined BLASTp, CDS extraction, and IGV visualization steps helped confirm gene structure and highlight differences in genome assembly quality among species.

Overall, this work showed how comparative genomics can be used to trace essential genes like BRCA2 across related species, and it reinforces the importance of high-quality genomic resources for accurate gene identification. As more complete primate genomes become available, future work could refine these findings and better resolve BRCA2 evolution across the primate lineage.
